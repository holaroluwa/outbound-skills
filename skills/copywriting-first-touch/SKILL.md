# Copywriting: First Touch

The first email's only job is to earn a reply. Short, about them, led by a real signal, asking for almost nothing. This skill writes that.

## Input needed
- Prospect name and company.
- The **signal**: the specific, true reason you're reaching out now (a hire, a post, a launch, a trigger).
- The offer angle, or read the offer and pains from `outreach-brief.md`.

If there's no real signal, say so. A fake "loved your post" opener is worse than no opener.

## Method
1. Open on the signal, in their world, not yours. The first line is about them.
2. Bridge to one pain (section 6 of the brief) in their own language.
3. One sentence of relevant proof or value, not the whole offer.
4. A low-friction CTA: a question or "worth a look?", never "book a 30-minute demo."
5. Keep the body under 75 words. Cut every word that isn't pulling weight.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)
- No em-dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.
- No corporate filler: no "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "leverage", "synergy", "circle back", "touch base", "add value".
- Short sentences. Write like a human who respects their time. Specific over smooth.

## Output
- 3 subject-line options (under 5 words, lowercase is fine, curiosity or specificity, no clickbait).
- The email body, under 75 words.
- One line naming the signal used, so it's easy to swap per prospect.
